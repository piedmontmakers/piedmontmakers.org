On Saturday, November 14, Highlander Robotics students welcomed girls in grades 2-3 to the team's [10th Street practice field](/facilities#tenth-street) in Oakland for a Nightlight Design workshop. The girls learned how electronic circuits work, then designed and built their own nightlights while Girl Scouts worked toward their Create and Innovate badge.

Tristen designed and led the workshop, with Zayda, Ameliese, Sam B., Alex, Mason, and Shane running it alongside. Thank you, all of you. FTC Team 37264 brought their robot to show the girls and let each of them take a turn driving it on the practice field, which was a big hit. Thank you, 37264!

Engagement was high all around, and plenty of parents asked how their kids can join robotics. For girls in grades 2-3, the place to start is LEGO League Explore. Our [robotics page](/robotics) covers every level, from kindergarten through high school.

## High schoolers passing it on

Highlander Robotics students write the presentations and build the materials for these workshops themselves. It's one of several ways Teams 8033 and 8433 give back to younger kids: the team also mentors local LEGO League teams and runs a-CAD-emy, a summer camp that teaches middle schoolers to design in Onshape CAD.

Workshops like this give young girls an early, hands-on start as problem solvers and inventors, taught by teenagers they can picture becoming. An afternoon wiring up a nightlight is a small thing, and it's often how a lifelong interest in STEAM begins.

## A practice field for the whole region

10th Street is the only community-accessible full-size FRC field in the Bay Area. Highlander Robotics built it and runs it, and Piedmont Makers holds the lease as the team's fiscal sponsor. In its first six months, 32 teams across FRC, FTC, and LEGO League logged more than 1,300 practice hours there.

Days like this one show the space is about more than competition practice. The same floor where high school teams tune their robots is where a second grader builds her first circuit.
