Piedmont Unified district leadership is hosting a community meeting to propose updates to the secondary math pathways, the course sequences students follow from 6th through 12th grade. The proposal also sets up the adoption of a new math curriculum. Staff and community members are all welcome, and you can attend in person or online.

- **When:** Tuesday, October 13, 4:30 to 6:00 pm
- **Where:** Ellen Driscoll Theater at Havens Elementary
- **Online:** [join on Zoom](https://us02web.zoom.us/j/85672175174?pwd=1GVVA8egbS5dnPYsw2tHTDTKwFzwKv.1)

If you care about STEAM education in Piedmont's schools, we encourage you to attend and help shape how the next generation learns math.

## Two decisions in one meeting

The pathways decide which math courses PUSD offers, in what order, and who can move faster. They determine, for example, whether a student can reach calculus before graduating. The curriculum is the textbooks and materials teachers use in class every day. The meeting covers the pathways first, with the curriculum adoption to follow, so feedback now shapes what students will learn from for years.

## How math works at PUSD today

According to [Piedmont Middle School's math program page](https://pms.piedmont.k12.ca.us/academics-programs/math-program), most students take Common Core 6, 7, and 8 in middle school, then Integrated Math 1 in 9th grade. Students who want to accelerate have two options:

- **Middle school compression (4 years in 3):** starting in 6th grade, students take combined courses (CC6/7, CC7/8, then CC8/Math 1) and arrive at Piedmont High one year ahead.
- **High school compression (3 years in 2):** students on the grade-level path in middle school can compress Integrated Math 1 through 3 into 9th and 10th grade.

Either route leads to [AP Calculus by senior year](https://phs.piedmont.k12.ca.us/academics-programs/mathematics). Piedmont High's upper-level courses include Math Analysis, Statistics, AP Calculus AB, and AP Calculus BC, with honors sections of Math Analysis and Statistics. The district's current guidance is cautious about acceleration: if there is any doubt, it recommends the grade-level pathway.

Pathway decisions have drawn strong community interest before. The school board adopted a new set of pathways on a [3-2 vote in 2018](https://www.piedmontcivic.org/2018/03/11/opinion-problematic-newly-approved-math-pathways-program/) after a contentious public debate, and in 2023 the parent group ALPS hosted a forum to [demystify the pathways](https://piedmontexedra.com/2023/10/demystifying-pusd-math-pathways-topic-of-oct-19-forum) for families.

## Why it matters for young makers

Nearly everything our students build depends on math. FTC and FRC robotics teams use geometry and trigonometry to plan robot motion, and the after-school engineering programs in the Engineering Lab at Piedmont High lean on algebra constantly. When the school board approved calculus-based [AP Physics C](/teacher-grants) for Piedmont High after years of advocacy from parents, students, and Piedmont Makers, the math sequence behind it became even more important: students need calculus in time to take it.

We haven't seen the proposal yet and aren't taking a position on it. We want families who care about STEAM to hear it firsthand and give the district their feedback.

## Questions you might bring

- What changes are proposed for each grade, and when would they take effect?
- How would the changes affect students already on a compression pathway?
- Would every student still have a route to calculus, and to AP Physics C, by senior year?
- How will the new curriculum be chosen, and will teachers pilot options in class first?
- How can families who can't attend send feedback afterward?

See you on October 13.
