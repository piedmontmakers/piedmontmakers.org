On Saturday, November 14, about 60 FIRST LEGO League Challenge teams came to Piedmont Middle School for a judged practice of the presentation side of the competition. Each team brought two posters, one for its Innovation Project and one for its Robot Design, and presented both to a panel of adult judges.

At a LEGO League tournament, kids present entirely on their own, with no help from coaches or parents. This practice gave teams a chance to stand in front of judges and answer their questions before it counts. Every team went home with scores and feedback from the judges, which leaves three weeks to sharpen their projects and presentations before the [Community Tournament](/calendar#event-2026-12-05-fll-challenge-founders-tournament) on December 5.

## Biodiversity, from wildfires to cow burps

This season's Innovation Project theme is biodiversity, and the range of ideas was wide. One team designed drones that put out wildfires autonomously. Another proposed a five-story shrimp hatchery to protect mangrove trees. Another took on cow burps and the methane they add to the atmosphere.

For Robot Design, teams walked the judges through how they built and programmed the robots they'll run in the robot game.

We were impressed by the creativity in every room, and we can't wait to see how teams use the judges' feedback. Thanks to all the adults who volunteered as judges and helped run the day.

## Coming up

The season wraps up with the [Community Tournament](/calendar#event-2026-12-05-fll-challenge-founders-tournament) on Saturday, December 5, at Piedmont Middle School, where teams run the robot game and present to judges for awards.
